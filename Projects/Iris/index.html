Iris: Empowering Video MLLMs with High-Frequency Pose Priors via Spatiotemporal Binding

TL;DR

Iris pairs sparse RGB frames with dense human pose sequences and binds them in time and space. These compact motion cues improve fine-grained action understanding and repetition counting with little additional MLLM inference cost.

Overview

Abstract

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated remarkable effectiveness in general video understanding. Human motion understanding remains a bottleneck due to the lack of explicit structural cues in visual patches and the motion information lost through sparse temporal sampling. We introduce Iris (Integrating representations of in-the-wild skeletons), a pose-augmented video MLLM for human-centric motion understanding. Iris pairs a sparse vision stream with a lightweight, high-frequency human pose stream to provide spatially structured and temporally dense kinematic signals. A cross-modal spatiotemporal binding mechanism uses pose-structured 3D RoPE for temporal alignment and person-grounded cross-attention for spatial visual grounding. An automated pose curation pipeline supports large-scale training with a heterogeneous pose representation. Experiments demonstrate improved performance on fine-grained motion understanding and repetitive action counting, while preserving general video understanding with efficient token computation.

Method

Sparse visual context, dense motion priors

16 RGB frames provide visual context; 160 pose frames preserve fine-grained motion.

  1. Encode dense poses compactly

    A heterogeneous descriptor combines 2D coordinates, local 3D articulation, pelvis translation, and visibility. A pose encoder produces five semantic body-part tokens per person and effective visual time step.

  2. Bind pose and vision in time and space

    Parallel 3D RoPE gives corresponding pose and visual tokens shared temporal indices. Person-grounded cross-attention connects part-aware pose queries to the corresponding person's visual region.

  3. Train progressively

    Pose–text contrastive pretraining initializes the motion encoder. Proxy tasks establish visual correspondence, followed by supervised fine-tuning with pose-paired and RGB-only examples.
